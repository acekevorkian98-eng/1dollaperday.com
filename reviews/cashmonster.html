CashMonster Review 2026: Crypto Surveys, Offers and a $5 First Payout

Category: Crypto GPT

Unproven

CashMonster is a new-looking rewards site that pays in crypto for surveys, offerwalls, PTC ads, daily quests and a lottery-style spin called MonsterDrop. It offers a $0.50 sign-up bonus, a $5 first withdrawal that gets a one-time review, and a 10% lifetime referral share. It publishes no earning rates, no payout totals and no operator name, and we found no independent reviews or payment proofs. Treat it as unproven: free to try, but cash out the first $5 before putting in any real time.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by CashMonster and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from CashMonster's public landing page, checked on 2 November 2026, and a search that found no independent reviews. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public page.

What is CashMonster?

CashMonster (cashmonster.app) is a get-paid-to site that routes you to third-party survey and offer providers and pays you in crypto. It sells itself as "eight ways to earn" and adds game mechanics on top: daily quests, a VIP program with 50 levels, and MonsterDrop reel spins that award lottery and jackpot tickets.

Ways to Earn

  • Surveys: from partners it names as CPX Research and PrimeSurvey.
  • Offer walls: try new apps and games or finish simple tasks.
  • PTC ads: view short ads for a few crypto units each.
  • Daily quests, VIP levels and MonsterDrop: bonus rewards and ticket multipliers from 1x to 10x.
  • Referrals: 10% of what your invitees make, which the site says lasts forever.

CashMonster publishes no rate for any of these. On most PTC sites an ad is worth roughly $0.001, so reaching a $5 withdrawal from ads alone would take thousands of views, and surveys and offers are what get you there faster. Our offerwall strategy guide explains which offers are worth the time.

Getting Paid

  • Method: cryptocurrency only. The page says "10+" coins but lists eight: BTC, ETH, LTC, DOGE, USDT, SOL, BNB and TRX.
  • Minimum: $5 for the first withdrawal. Later minimums aren't stated.
  • Review: the first withdrawal gets a "one-time review", after which "trusted accounts" are paid automatically. The criteria aren't explained.
  • Fees: the network fee is shown before you confirm, but no amount or schedule is published.

A fee that's only visible at checkout can take a large bite out of a $5 withdrawal on a high-fee network, so choose a cheap one such as TRX or SOL if the option is there. See our cash-out minimums and fees guide for how to compare.

What We Couldn't Verify

  • No operator, company name or address, only a 2026 copyright line.
  • No site age, user count or total paid out.
  • No rules on VPNs or multiple accounts, which cause most bans on this kind of site.
  • No independent reviews or payment proofs in our search.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Free to join, with a $0.50 sign-up bonus
  • Low $5 first withdrawal
  • Uses established survey partners
  • Several coins, including low-fee options

Cons

  • No published earning rates
  • Operator unnamed and no track record we can find
  • Manual review on the first withdrawal
  • Lottery and ticket mechanics push you to keep playing
  • "10+ coins" claim doesn't match the eight listed

Tips if You Try It

  1. Use one account and no VPN. The rules aren't published, so assume they're strict.
  2. Aim for the $5 first. Request it and see whether it arrives before you invest more time.
  3. Pick a low-fee coin for the first withdrawal.
  4. Skip the lottery tickets. They aren't earnings.
  5. Don't pay for anything. Nothing on the page requires a deposit, and nothing should.

Final Verdict

Verdict: unproven, free to test

CashMonster looks like a polished survey and offerwall front end with a $5 starting line, but it names no operator and shows no proof of payment. Try it for the free bonus, request the first $5, and only then decide whether it deserves more of your day.
