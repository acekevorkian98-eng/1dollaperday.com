Pionex Review 2026: A Trading-Bot Exchange With a Multistate Consent Order and a 2.5 Trustpilot Score

Category: Crypto Exchange and Bots

Not an Earner

Pionex is a crypto exchange known for built-in trading bots (grid, DCA and similar). It isn't a micro-earning site: you must deposit and trade, and you can lose money. We couldn't view its own site (it sits behind a Cloudflare security screen), so this review rests on two public records. First, a consent order published by South Dakota's banking regulator, signed by four US states, says an examination found Pionex failed to collect required sender and beneficiary details on transfers over $3,000, and that it had operated in North Carolina and Ohio before it was licensed there. It carries a $20,000 penalty and Pionex admits no wrongdoing. Second, Trustpilot shows a 2.5 score from 637 reviews, split 48% five-star and 40% one-star, with complaints about withdrawals, KYC restrictions and support. Treat it as a trading venue, not an earner.

What is Pionex?

Pionex runs a spot exchange with a set of automated trading bots, including grid and dollar-cost-averaging bots, and a debit card product. Pionex Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, and the order identifies it as a licensed money transmitter in the participating states. Trustpilot lists a contact location in Panama for the profile. We have not been able to load pionex.com, which sits behind a Cloudflare security screen we couldn't pass, so we haven't verified its current fees, products or terms.

What the Consent Order Says

A consent order is a settlement with regulators. According to the document, the states of Nebraska, North Carolina, Ohio and South Dakota examined Pionex between November and December 2024, covering August 2022 to September 2024, and issued a report on 10 March 2025. It cited:

  • A federal Funds Transfer Rule violation: failing to collect sender and beneficiary information on transactions over $3,000.
  • State compliance deficiencies, including an ineffective information security program, failure to maintain permissible investments and adequate surety bond coverage, record-keeping failures, and late annual and quarterly reports.
  • Unlicensed money transmission before it was licensed: in North Carolina, 241 new customers made deposits between 13 May and 4 August 2022, and in Ohio at least 612 transactions totalling at least $293,000 occurred before its licence on 31 October 2022.

Pionex agreed to quarterly progress reports for two years and a $20,000 administrative penalty ($10,000 each to North Carolina and Ohio). The order says Pionex "does not admit any wrongdoing" and that it has implemented procedures to comply. The order is also a disciplinary action that Pionex must disclose on future licence filings.

That's a compliance record, not evidence that user funds were lost or misused. The order doesn't say that, and a $20,000 penalty is small. It does tell you the regulators found weaknesses in security, record-keeping and reserve-style requirements at the time of the examination, which matters for a place that holds your money.

What Users Say

Pionex's Trustpilot profile has been claimed since December 2020. The score is 2.5 from 637 reviews, 62 of them in the last year, with 48% five-star and 40% one-star and little in between. Trustpilot adds that the company "may be associated with high-risk investments" and hasn't invited reviews recently.

  • Complaints: withdrawals stuck or rejected, funds frozen after deposits or profits, new KYC demands and trading restrictions without notice, deposits sent on unsupported networks that cost a recovery fee, card charges after freezing a card, losses blamed on bots or an app freeze during liquidation, and slow support.
  • Praise: a smooth interface, easy bots, fast support for some, and months of profitable bot use for others.
  • Company replies: it replies to 94% of negative reviews, typically citing policy or compliance, disputing claims, or saying it couldn't find a matching case. Trustpilot doesn't verify the claims.

Why It Isn't an Earner

Trading bots don't create income: they automate buying and selling, so results depend on the market, and you can lose your deposit. Any "bot profit" figure you see is a result, not a rate. Tips if You Try It

  1. Check your own country's rules. The order concerns US state licensing; availability and protection vary.
  2. Never deposit more than you can lose, and withdraw profits regularly.
  3. Send deposits only on supported networks. Recovery of mistakes costs a fee.
  4. Complete KYC early and keep records of your trades.

Final Verdict

Verdict: a trading venue with a compliance record, not an earner

Pionex has a real corporate footprint and some satisfied users, but it also has a multistate consent order, a low Trustpilot score full of withdrawal and KYC complaints, and it can lose you money. It doesn't belong in a $1-a-day routine. If you use it anyway, keep balances small and read its current terms yourself.
