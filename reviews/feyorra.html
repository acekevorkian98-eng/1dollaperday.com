Feyorra Review 2026: A FaucetPay Faucet With Very Small Payouts

Category: Faucet and PTC

Unproven

Feyorra is a free faucet, PTC and task site that pays automatically to FaucetPay in nine coins. It claims 116.9K members, $63,547 paid out and 115.5K completed payouts, which is about 55 cents a payout. Its own live-withdrawals feed tells a smaller story: the eight rows we captured ranged from about $0.002 to $0.08. It doesn't publish its minimum withdrawals or name an operator, and we found no independent reviews. A reasonable free add-on to a FaucetPay routine, with small expectations.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by Feyorra and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from Feyorra's public home page and a search that found no independent reviews, checked on 2 November 2026. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public pages.

What is Feyorra?

Feyorra (feyorra.site) is a get-paid-to site built around FaucetPay. You can claim a faucet every minute, watch PTC ads, do "Video Earn" tasks (YouTube view, like and subscribe), complete shortlinks and offers, and collect a daily bonus and raffle. Everything lands in one balance and is withdrawn automatically to your FaucetPay account once you reach the coin's minimum.

What the Payouts Look Like

The homepage counters say $63,547 paid to members across 115.5K completed payouts. That averages about $0.55 a payout. The live withdrawals feed below it shows something smaller:

The eight rows we captured add up to roughly 13 cents, an average of about 1.7 cents. That fits how faucets work: you're paid in fractions of a cent per claim and withdraw once you pass a low minimum. It also means the all-time average is probably pulled up by a few larger payouts. Our real hourly rate guide shows why a few cents a day is the realistic scale for sites like this.

Rules That Get Accounts Locked

  • One account per person, with no shared IP or network.
  • Multiple accounts are banned and balances forfeited.
  • VPNs, proxies, scripts and auto-clickers are blocked and accounts using them are locked.

The footer warns that earnings "depend on activity and are not guaranteed", and the minimum withdrawal per coin isn't published on the public pages. Check it in your account before you start.

Who Runs It?

No operator, company or address is given, only a 2026 copyright line, and we found no independent reviews or payment proofs for feyorra.site. Searches for "Feyorra" turn up material about the FEY token on FaucetPay, which is a different thing. The site earns from FaucetPay sign-ups through its own referral links, which is normal for this category but worth knowing.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Free to join, with no deposit
  • Automatic withdrawals to FaucetPay in nine coins
  • Several earning types in one balance
  • Live withdrawals feed shows real, if tiny, payouts
  • 30% lifetime referral commission

Cons

  • Payouts are fractions of a cent to a few cents
  • Minimum withdrawals not published
  • No named operator and no independent reviews
  • Strict anti-VPN and anti-script rules
  • Video tasks depend on YouTube actions

Tips if You Try It

  1. Use one account and no VPN.
  2. Check the minimum for your coin before you start claiming.
  3. Pick a coin with a low minimum such as LTC, TRX or USDT.
  4. Withdraw the first balance early to prove the payout route.
  5. Keep it to a few minutes a day. At this scale, time is the cost.

For a setup that works the same way, see our FireFaucet review and the FaucetPay guide.

Final Verdict

Verdict: unproven, tiny but plausible

Feyorra looks like a working, FaucetPay-based faucet whose live payouts are real but very small. With no operator and no outside reviews, treat it as an experiment: claim for a week, withdraw the first balance, and keep it only if the numbers justify your time.
