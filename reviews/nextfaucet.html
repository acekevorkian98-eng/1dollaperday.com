NextFaucet Review 2026: A Bitcoin Cash Faucet That Paid About a Cent Per User, Now a Blog

Category: Crypto Faucet (Offline)

Offline

NextFaucet (nextfaucet.com) was a free faucet with magic-link login and no passwords. The Internet Archive's copy from 2025 shows a Bitcoin Lightning faucet with 458 users and 703 sats paid. By its last working capture, on 9 May 2026, it had become a Bitcoin Cash faucet with 23,543 users, 5,439,386 claims and 0.76023327 BCH paid in total. At today's CoinGecko price of $277.55 per BCH, that is about $211 across all users: roughly 0.9 cents per user and about four thousandths of a cent per claim. Its recent withdrawals ranged from 0.0001 to 0.0003 BCH, about 3 to 9 cents. Today nextfaucet.com serves an unrelated blog called Blocknote, and we found no closure notice.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by NextFaucet and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from the Internet Archive's saved copies of nextfaucet.com from 2025 and 9 May 2026, and a live capture of the domain today, checked on 2 November 2026. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public pages.

What is NextFaucet?

NextFaucet billed itself as a "next-generation" faucet with zero fees and "no passwords": you entered an email, solved a captcha and received a login link. The 2025 copy offered Bitcoin Lightning payouts, level multipliers and a 50% referral bonus. The May 2026 copy had switched to Bitcoin Cash, with a manual faucet, an auto faucet, scratch cards, a leaderboard, a YouTube channel and "RefActive", which pays +2 sats an hour for each referral who claims at least once every 24 hours.

Referrals and RefActive

The standard referral program paid 50% of every claim your referrals made. RefActive added +2 sats an hour per active referral once you'd accumulated 10 sats. At 2 sats an hour a referral is worth about 48 sats a day, a fraction of a cent, so the scheme rewards bringing people in more than anything else.

Where It Is Now

The Internet Archive's last working capture of the faucet is dated 9 May 2026. Today the domain serves "Blocknote", a general blockchain blog. We found no closure notice and no word on balances. A faucet domain that turns into a content blog is a common end for abandoned sites, and it means any remaining balance is out of reach. See our warning signs guide.

  1. Withdraw small balances often. This is the lesson of a faucet that vanishes.
  2. Don't trust a domain you haven't checked. Expired faucet domains get reused.
  3. Keep payout records.
