PiTasks Review 2026: A $0.01 Minimum Payout, and What Sits Behind It

Category: Faucet, PTC and Offers

Unproven

PiTasks combines a faucet, PTC, micro-tasks, offers, surveys, games and timed site surfing, with a $0.01 minimum payout and a long list of ways to cash out: FaucetPay, Binance, Cwallet, PayPal, BTC, SOL and USDT. Its own counters say 10K+ users, $9K+ paid and 7 months online, which is under a dollar paid per user. Trustpilot has a positive but thin profile (4.4 from 18 recent reviews, none reporting a missing payout), though one reviewer says it was down for months and Trustpilot warns of possible association with high-risk investments. Worth a small test, with a tiny first withdrawal.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by PiTasks and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from PiTasks' public home page, its Trustpilot profile and a search for independent reviews, checked on 2 November 2026. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public pages.

What is PiTasks?

PiTasks (pitasks.com) is a get-paid-to site in the CoinPayU and RewardJoy mould: faucet claims, PTC tasks, micro-tasks, offers, game installs, surveys, timed website surfing and YouTube watching. You can sign up with an email, Google or Telegram. Its logo and some branding refer to "RewardJoy", the name CoinPayU now operates under, which suggests it runs on the same kind of white-label platform.

The Numbers It Shows

PiTasks shows 10K+ users, $9K+ in rewards paid, 97% "instant delivery" and "7 months+" online, plus a "rated Excellent by 123 users, score 4.8" badge. $9,000 over 10,000 users is under a dollar each, so the average user has earned very little. The offers on display (a $150 game offer, an $85 game offer) are the usual "reach level X" targets that few people hit, and no conditions are shown. Our offerwall strategy guide explains why most of those never pay.

Getting Paid

  • Minimum: "minimum payout only 0.01$", claimed on the home page.
  • Methods: FaucetPay, Binance, Cwallet, PayPal and crypto (BTC, SOL, USDT). The list of coins is long, but the page doesn't say which methods have which minimums.
  • Fees: none mentioned.

A $0.01 minimum is easy to test. Check what fee your chosen method charges before you rely on it, using our cash-out minimums and fees guide.

What Trustpilot Shows

PiTasks' Trustpilot profile is claimed, with a 4.4 score from 18 reviews in the last twelve months, dated January to March 2026, and 83% of them five stars. Reviewers write things like "Binance withdrawal amazing" and "just withdrew, 100% real", and no visible review reports an unpaid payout. Against that, one reviewer who said they were paid later cut their rating to two stars because the site "has been down for months". Another says a friend asked them to write their review. Trustpilot notes the company hasn't invited reviews recently and adds a warning that it "may be associated with high-risk investments". The company hasn't replied to the negative review.

Referrals

A three-level affiliate program pays 5%, 7% and 10%, with higher account levels earning a higher percentage. Only the first level is a normal referral reward. Don't build a plan around the deeper ones.

What We Couldn't Verify

  • No operator, company or address on the public pages.
  • The FAQ, terms and privacy pages weren't readable to us, so the rules on VPNs, multiple accounts and fees are unknown.
  • No independent reviews beyond Trustpilot, and no verifiable payment proofs.

Tips if You Try It

  1. Make the first withdrawal tiny and see which method arrives.
  2. Ignore the big game offers. Level targets rarely pay.
  3. Use one account and no VPN since the rules aren't visible.
  4. Read the terms once you're signed in.
  5. Don't deposit or buy anything.

Check our warning signs an earning site is about to stop paying if the site goes quiet.

Final Verdict

Verdict: unproven, plausible, test small

PiTasks has a low minimum, a wide choice of payout routes and a positive but thin Trustpilot record. It also has no named operator and a report of long downtime. Test it with a tiny withdrawal, avoid the large game offers, and put real time elsewhere until it has a longer record.
