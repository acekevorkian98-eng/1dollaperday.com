PepeCore Review 2026: A PEPE “Mining Platform” Promising 200% in 24 Hours, Now Offline

Category: Cloud Mining (Offline)

Do Not Deposit

PepeCore (pepecore.one) called itself the 'number one crypto mining platform' and promised to 'earn 200% profits in just 24 hrs' and 'up to 50% daily returns', with 'instant payouts via FaucetPay'. We could not load it on 2 November 2026: the host returns an SSL handshake error (Cloudflare 525). The review below is from the copy the Internet Archive saved on 9 July 2026. A 200% return in a day, or 50% every day, can't come from mining PEPE or anything else, and returns like that are the classic sign of a scheme that pays old users from new deposits. We have not tested it. We recommend you never deposit in anything that advertises returns like these.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by PepeCore and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from the copy of pepecore.one saved by the Internet Archive on 9 July 2026, since the live site shows a host error, checked on 2 November 2026. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public pages.

What is PepeCore?

PepeCore asked visitors to log in with a FaucetPay email or PEPE address ("register instantly"), offered a free 50 GH/s mining bonus, and showed a "live mining demo", described on the page as a real-time simulation of PEPE growth. It also had stats, a profit calculator, payouts and FAQ sections, and described itself as "100% protected" and "100% secure access". The pages we saw gave no company name, address or terms.

The Claims

  • "Earn 200% profits in just 24 hrs". That's paying out triple your money in a day.
  • "Up to 50% daily returns". At 50% a day compounded, $10 would become about $577 after ten days and about $1.9 million after thirty, which is why no real business offers it.
  • "Instant payouts via FaucetPay". No minimum, fee or schedule is shown.
  • "100% protected". No audit, insurance or licence is cited.

Compare this with the claims we tested in our AiBio.Store review (12% a day) and the slow contracts in our BeMine review (a payback of 1.2 to 3.2 years). Returns that outrun both by orders of magnitude aren't funded by mining. When a platform pays that much, the likeliest source is newer members' deposits, and those schemes tend to stop paying suddenly. See our warning signs guide.

The Stats It Showed

The strip read 13,516 members, 314,300,747 deposited, 32,563,671 withdrawn and 4 days online (units aren't stated, and presumably are PEPE tokens). Withdrawals were about 10% of deposits, and "4 days online" on a site the archive first saved in April 2026 suggests the counter wasn't a real clock. We can't verify any of these figures.

Where It Is Now

On 2 November 2026 pepecore.one returns a Cloudflare "SSL handshake failed" page: Cloudflare is working, but the origin server isn't completing a secure connection. That could be a temporary fault, but combined with the claims above, we wouldn't treat it as one. If you have a balance, withdraw it only if the site works again, and never "top up" to unlock it.

Tips if You Try It

  1. Don't deposit.
  2. Never "invest" to unlock a withdrawal. That is a standard trick when a scheme stops paying.
  3. Never share your FaucetPay login details with a site that asks for the email only to "register instantly" if you can avoid it.
  4. Be wary of look-alike domains if the site resurfaces.

If you have already deposited, keep your records and don't send more money to recover it.

Final Verdict

Verdict: do not deposit

PepeCore promised returns no mining operation, faucet or ad budget can fund, named no operator, and its host now shows an error. We haven't tested a withdrawal, so this is a judgment on the claims, but it's a firm one: stay away.
