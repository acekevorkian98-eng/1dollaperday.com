Userlytics Review 2026: What Testers Get Paid, When, and the Terms to Read First

Category: Website Testing

Occasional Extra

Userlytics is a real user-experience research company that pays panel testers to try websites, apps and prototypes while thinking aloud on camera. Its tester page advertises $10 to $50 per study, paid only through PayPal, and typical tests take 20 to 40 minutes. The catch: invitations are random and can't be requested, and the terms list several situations in which a completed test is not paid. Treat it as an occasional bonus next to other testing panels, not a daily income.

What is Userlytics?

Userlytics sells remote user-experience research to companies. A business that wants to know where people get stuck on its checkout, app or new prototype pays Userlytics to recruit testers and record them using it. Testers are the supply side of that service.

As a tester you join the Userlytics panel, fill in a demographic profile, take a short practice test, and wait for an email invitation when a study matches your profile. Each study shows its reward, approximate length, study type and required device before you start.

How a Userlytics Test Works

  1. Invitation. You get an email when a client's study fits your profile. Userlytics says it can't predict, influence or guarantee how often you'll be invited, and asking support for more tests won't change that.
  2. Setup. On desktop you install the Userlytics recorder through a Chrome or Edge extension. On mobile you use the iOS or Android app.
  3. The test. You follow the client's tasks, for example "search for jeans and go to checkout, but stop before the order is confirmed", and talk constantly about what you see, expect and find confusing. Some studies record your webcam, and your face must be clearly visible.
  4. Rating and approval. The client rates your feedback. Userlytics says clients want critical feedback, not praise. A positively rated test is marked approved.
  5. Payment. Approved tests are paid automatically to your PayPal email on a fortnightly Friday cycle.

Some studies are surveys or live, moderated conversations rather than self-guided recordings. The app labels the type before you start.

Equipment Needed

  • Mac (OS X 10.11 or later) or Windows 7+ PC with Chrome or Edge
  • Headphones and a working microphone
  • A webcam for some studies
  • For mobile studies: iPhone on iOS 16+ or Android 9+
  • A quiet room

How Much Can You Actually Earn?

It depends almost entirely on how many invitations you get, and Userlytics says outright that it can't predict that.

What Userlytics does publish:

  • Per study: $10 to $50, shown on the tester sign-up page.
  • Per test time: typically 20 to 40 minutes.

What it doesn't publish is how many studies an average tester receives, or what share of invitations end in a paid, approved test. We haven't taken paid tests ourselves, so we won't invent a monthly figure. Be wary of any review that quotes a confident monthly Userlytics income without showing the payment history behind it.

What you can influence is your chance of being invited. Userlytics says answering profile surveys and completing your tester profile helps.

When and How You Get Paid

According to Userlytics' help centre (updated 17 December 2025):

  • Payments go out automatically to PayPal, every two weeks on a Friday.
  • Payment is made on the first Friday after a 15-day window following approval. A test approved on the 1st of the month is paid on the first Friday after the 16th.
  • Your dashboard shows the relevant date under "Revision Date".

The tester terms are looser: they say you are paid "a maximum of 30 days after fully completing a test." Plan around the 30-day limit and treat the 15-day cycle as the usual case.

PayPal is the only option. Userlytics says it has no other payment method at the moment. Testers are paid in US dollars, except those in the EU and UK, who are paid in euros. If PayPal doesn't operate in your country, or you can't withdraw from it easily, Userlytics isn't for you. PayPal's conversion and withdrawal fees vary by country, so check PayPal's fee page for yours before counting on the full amount.

The Fine Print: When a Test Isn't Paid

This is the section most reviews skip. Userlytics' tester terms say it may deny payment for:

  • incorrect or misleading profile information
  • a poor setup, such as background noise, no audio, poor sound quality or a blocked webcam
  • duplicate accounts
  • "substandard testing"
  • expired tests, and results that never reached Userlytics, "whatever the reason"
  • incomplete reports, such as not following the tasks or not speaking your thoughts aloud

Two more terms matter:

  • Client-recruited studies may be unpaid. If a client invited you directly, for example a company testing with its own customers, and chose not to pay, Userlytics won't pay you either.
  • Upload failures count against you. Results that were never received are unpaid. Userlytics publishes recovery guides for failed uploads on iOS and Android, so read those before your first mobile test.

One account per person

Duplicate accounts are removed along with their earnings. Running extra accounts for more invitations is the quickest way to lose everything you've earned.

Privacy: What You're Agreeing To

Usability testing means being recorded. Before signing up, know that:

  • Clients can see your demographic profile, but not your full name.
  • Userlytics may use portions of your recordings (screen, audio, webcam and written comments) in samples for potential clients or in marketing material. Its help centre says that if you don't want that, you shouldn't sign up.
  • You agree to keep what you test confidential, including prototypes.
  • Never enter real financial or personal data in a test. If a test asks for a card number, ID number, password or home address, Userlytics says to stop and contact support.
  • Userlytics says it is ISO 27001 certified and follows GDPR.
  • You can delete your account from Settings. Userlytics says full removal can take 48 to 72 hours.
  • Disputes go to binding arbitration (American Arbitration Association, Florida, USA).

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Real research company: clients pay for studies and testers are paid from that
  • $10 to $50 per study advertised, with the reward shown before you start
  • Fixed, automatic PayPal payout cycle
  • Open wherever PayPal works
  • The reasons a test can be rejected are written down

Cons

  • Invitations are random and asking doesn't help
  • PayPal is the only payout method
  • Payout can take up to 30 days under the terms
  • Completed tests can still go unpaid for setup or quality reasons, or a failed upload
  • Recordings may be used in marketing

Who Userlytics Suits

A good fit if you already use PayPal and can withdraw from it in your country, are comfortable thinking aloud (and on webcam when required), have a quiet room with headphones and a decent mic, and are happy to treat each study as a bonus.

A poor fit if you need predictable daily earnings, don't have PayPal, dislike being recorded, or don't want clips of you possibly used in Userlytics' marketing.

Tips if You Sign Up

  1. Complete every profile survey. Userlytics says this improves your chance of being invited.
  2. Whitelist no-reply-invitations@userlytics.com so invitations don't land in spam.
  3. Test your mic and webcam before accepting a study. Poor audio and blocked webcams are listed reasons for non-payment.
  4. Talk the whole time and be critical. Silence and vague praise are the fastest route to a low rating.
  5. Never use real card details or passwords. Stop the test and contact support if asked.
  6. On mobile, read the upload-recovery guide first. A test that never uploads isn't paid.
  7. Track approval dates. If an approved test passes 30 days without payment, contact Userlytics support with the test details.

Userlytics vs Similar Testing Sites

Userlytics works much like the other testing panels we've reviewed. Signing up for several is the usual way to get regular invitations.

  • UserTesting: the largest panel, with live sessions as well as recorded tests.
  • Userfeel: a similar think-aloud format.
  • Respondent and dscout: fewer, longer and better-paid research studies.

Final Verdict

Verdict: legitimate, but an occasional extra

Userlytics is worth a sign-up if you already have PayPal, but it won't be a daily earner. The advertised $10 to $50 for a 20 to 40 minute study is real pay for real work. You can't control how often invitations arrive, though, and sound quality, missing narration and failed uploads can all mean no payment, so read the terms before your first test.
