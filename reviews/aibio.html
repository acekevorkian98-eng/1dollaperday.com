AiBio.Store Review 2026: A “Mining Game” Advertising 12% a Day

Category: Mining Game

Do Not Deposit

AiBio.Store is a crypto 'mining game' where you buy virtual machines with a deposit, from $1, and a robot 'mines 24/7'. Its page says a $1 machine earns an estimated up to $0.12 a day and a $100 machine up to $12 a day. That is 12% a day, which pays your money back in about eight days and compounds to over 4,000% a year. Real mining and real ad-funded rewards can't pay that. It also pays referral commissions ten levels deep, doesn't name its operator, and says in the small print that it is 'a game, not an investment'. We recommend you don't deposit. The free robot and free bonuses are the only part worth even looking at.

How we reviewed it

We have not been paid by AiBio.Store and this page has no referral link. Everything below comes from AiBio.Store's public home page, which includes its demo and its own disclaimer, checked on 2 November 2026. We have not signed up or withdrawn, so this review carries no score and no earnings claims. The screenshots are our own captures of the public pages.

What is AiBio.Store?

AiBio.Store (aibio.store) presents itself as a "crypto mining game with a real economy". You get a free robot, then buy "machines" with a crypto deposit (about ten coins are accepted, including USDT on TRC20 and BEP20, Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum and TRON). The machines are said to earn daily, you can withdraw to your own wallet, and you earn a share of what your "team" buys. A banner offers a free bonus every six hours and a chance to "win a machine worth $10,000", and a "Season 1" challenge advertises up to $5,146 per player.

These are the site's own "up to" figures. A 12% daily return is more than 4,000% over a year, simple, before compounding. Compare it with BeMine's advertised GPU contracts in our BeMine review, which were already slow at 1.2 to 3.2 years to pay back. No mining operation, faucet or ad budget funds returns of that size to retail buyers over time. When a site pays "returns" that high, the money paid to early users generally comes from what newer users deposit. We can't see AiBio's books, so we can't say that's what happens here, but the maths gives you every reason not to count on it.

The page's own words

It says earnings are "not guaranteed" and that it is "a game, not an investment". That's a legal disclaimer sitting next to a daily return figure and a referral plan.

Red Flags

  • A 12% daily return for every machine tier.
  • Referrals ten levels deep. You earn on what your team buys, which rewards recruiting. No commission rates are published.
  • A demo that "skips ahead 30 days" at 60x speed, so the dashboard you try first shows made-up results.
  • No operator, location or registration. The page links Terms, Privacy and an Earnings Disclosure that we couldn't read.
  • No withdrawal minimum or fees on the page, and no independent reviews.

What We Couldn't Verify

We haven't deposited or withdrawn, and the site's terms were not readable to us, so we can't say how or whether withdrawals work. Our rating rests on the published figures and structure, not on a failed payout.

Cons

  • Operator unnamed
  • Demo skips time and uses fake money
  • Withdrawal rules and referral rates not published

Tips if You Try It

  1. Don't deposit. If you try the free robot, treat it as a demo.
  2. Never recruit friends into a plan whose returns you can't explain.
  3. If you do put in money anyway, only an amount you're happy to lose entirely, and withdraw all profit immediately.
  4. Don't use your main wallet.

Read our warning signs an earning site is about to stop paying and our ZenMine review for how similar sites ended.

Final Verdict

Verdict: do not deposit

AiBio.Store advertises a daily return no real mining or ad budget can sustain, pays ten levels of referrals, hides its operator and shows a demo that fast-forwards. We have not seen a failed withdrawal, so this is a judgment on the structure and the maths, but it's a firm one: don't deposit.
